# Model Narrative: Corporate Innovation-Award Narrative (Morgan Stanley-style)

> Source: narrative and budget written by Neo Mangope (Fundraising &
> Grants Officer) for the Morgan Stanley Alliance for Children's Mental
> Health Innovation Awards, 2027 cycle ($100,000 ask). Full text is in
> `../drafts/2026-07-morgan-stanley-childrens-mental-health-innovation-awards.md`.
> Use this as a **model for future corporate innovation/challenge-award
> submissions** that score against named evaluation criteria (here:
> Innovation, Expertise, Impact, Potential), not as boilerplate to paste
> verbatim. This is the first GOODProjects submission built around a new,
> not-yet-launched pilot concept (GOOD Debrief) rather than an existing,
> already-running program, and it is the cleanest example on file of
> house style executed by a human writer rather than drafted from the
> canon.

## Structure this narrative uses (mirror this section order for criteria-scored corporate awards)

1. **Purpose Statement ("What This Grant Will Make Possible"):** opens
   with the concrete thing the grant funds (a named, branded pilot: "GOOD
   Debrief"), states the specific gap it closes in one sentence, and
   names the funding ask as "runway" rather than a permanent commitment.
   No throat-clearing about the organization first; the project comes
   first.
2. **Organizational Leadership & Community-Proximity Compass:** ties
   staff lived experience directly to the mechanism of the new project
   (violence interrupters are "the ones who see a child the day after a
   shooting"), then cites one hard, funder-agnostic proof metric (45%
   violent-crime reduction in PSA 105 vs. 12% citywide increase, MPD
   validated) as evidence the org is positioned to deliver this
   specific innovation, not just credible in general.
3. **Evaluation-criteria-mirrored section, one paragraph per criterion:**
   when a funder publishes named, weighted evaluation criteria (here:
   Innovation, Expertise, Impact, Potential), answer them as labeled
   subheadings in the funder's own order and wording, not folded into
   generic narrative prose. Each paragraph does one job only: Innovation
   explains what's new and why (contrasts with a "general-purpose
   wellness chatbot" to sharpen the distinction), Expertise names the
   credentialed partner and the exact division of labor between AI and
   clinician, Impact lists the specific metrics that will be tracked,
   Potential addresses replicability to other organizations/geographies
   since "Potential" in this funder's language means scalability, not
   just growth.
4. **Learning & Iteration Cycle (Gather / Understand / Apply):** a
   three-step, one-line-per-step loop naming what data is collected, who
   reviews it and how often, and the concrete downstream outputs (tool
   refinement, staff training, a field-facing practitioner brief). This
   pattern is reusable for any funder that wants to see a learning
   system, not just a service delivery plan.
5. **Model Overview as a three-layer table:** Trigger to AI Check-In to
   Clinical Escalation, one row per layer, "Component" and "How It
   Works" columns. Useful whenever a proposal describes a pipeline or
   pathway (referral pathway, service continuum, intervention sequence);
   a table reads faster than prose for a reviewer skimming many
   applications.
6. **Budget Justification, itemized to the dollar, reconciling exactly
   to the ask:** five line items (largest: AI Tool Development $35,000;
   smallest: Staff Training $8,000) summing to exactly $100,000, each
   with a one-line purpose. Opens with a bracketed compliance note
   restating the funder's own overhead rule ("Direct project costs only
   ... no institutional overhead included") rather than leaving the
   reviewer to check eligibility guidelines separately.
7. **Closing "Why This Fills an Unmet Need" section:** a single
   contrast-structure paragraph (most innovation addresses "the system"
   ... this addresses "the moment") that restates the thesis without
   repeating the opening paragraph's sentences. Good closing pattern for
   any narrative that opened with a purpose statement.

## Style notes distinct from GOODProjects' standard canon narratives

- **New-pilot framing, not track-record framing.** Most GOODProjects
  narratives lead with what's already running (GOODZone's numbers, FSP's
  enrollment). This one leads with what does not exist yet and treats
  the org's existing infrastructure (violence-interruption network, C3
  Wellness relationship) as the reason GOODProjects, specifically, is
  positioned to build it. Use this framing whenever the ask is genuinely
  a new pilot, not an expansion of an existing service.
- **Answer the funder's named criteria in the funder's own words and
  order.** Do not paraphrase "Innovation / Expertise / Impact /
  Potential" into GOODProjects' own section headers; when a funder
  publishes scoring criteria, the safest structure is to mirror them
  exactly as subheadings.
- **One brand name for the concept ("GOOD Debrief"), used consistently.**
  Naming the pilot once and reusing that exact name throughout (never
  "the tool," "the program," and "the initiative" interchangeably) reads
  as more concrete to a reviewer and is easier to fact-check across the
  document.
- **AI positioned explicitly as "a front door, not a replacement for
  care."** When a proposal involves an AI-assisted service touching a
  vulnerable population (here, children in crisis), state the human
  clinical backstop and escalation trigger explicitly and early; do not
  leave a funder to infer that a licensed professional is still in the
  loop.
- **No em or en dashes; commas or sentence breaks used instead**,
  consistent with the mandatory humanize pass in `PLAYBOOK.md` Part 8.

## Reusable facts anchored in this narrative

- GOODZone: 45% reduction in violent crime in PSA 105 vs. 12% citywide
  increase (MPD-validated). Reusable in any narrative citing GOODZone's
  headline violence-reduction outcome.
- C3 Wellness: GOODProjects' clinical/psychosocial care partner,
  confirmed in `../org_profile/02-programs.md` and
  `../org_profile/06-strategic-brief-2026.md`. Reusable as the named
  clinical partner for any trauma-informed or mental-health proposal
  needing a credentialed-provider backstop.
- Service geography for this narrative: James Creek, Syphax Gardens,
  Greenleaf (Southwest DC, PSA 105), consistent with GOODProjects'
  standard geography claims elsewhere in the canon.

## Funder-process facts worth carrying forward (Morgan Stanley Innovation Awards specifically)

- Submission is through a named third-party platform
  (`https://app-ms.babele.co/application-definition/8`), logged in via
  the organization's Grants@GOODProjects Google account, not a funder-run
  portal. Confirm login access early for any Morgan Stanley or
  babele.co-hosted application.
- Funds are restricted to direct project costs; no institutional
  overhead. Any Morgan Stanley Innovation Awards budget must exclude
  indirect/overhead line items entirely, unlike GOODProjects' standard
  10% MTDC indirect-rate budgets used elsewhere.
- Notification is not until spring 2027 for the 2026 cycle; this is a
  seed/pilot-runway award, not fast turnaround funding.
