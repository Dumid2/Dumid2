# Model Narrative: Portal Q&A-Style Corporate Award Application (Morgan Stanley-style)

> Source: Morgan Stanley Alliance for Children's Mental Health Innovation
> Awards, 2027 cycle ($100,000 ask), built directly against the live
> portal's actual field questions and character limits
> (`morganstanley.theonevalley.com`). Full text is in
> `../drafts/2026-07-morgan-stanley-childrens-mental-health-innovation-awards.md`.
> Anchor: GOODLearningHub's Mental Health Services Expansion (ages 14-18),
> layered on top of an existing Aim High/NY Life Foundation-funded
> clinical position. Use this as a **model for future portal-based
> applications with hard character limits**, not as boilerplate to paste
> verbatim.
>
> Note: an earlier draft of this opportunity was built around a
> different, unlaunched concept ("GOOD Debrief," an AI-assisted check-in
> tool) and a different portal (babele.co). That concept was superseded
> once the real portal and real anchor program surfaced. Lesson for
> future opportunities: don't lock in a narrative concept before
> confirming the actual application portal and the program it will
> anchor to; the funder brief alone is not enough.

## Structure this narrative uses (mirror this for any application built against a live portal's own field list)

1. **Answer each portal field exactly as its own unit, in the portal's
   own order, respecting its stated character limit.** Every answer in
   this application is written to land just under its cap (e.g.
   488/500, 1725/2500, 987/1500), not because shorter is worse but
   because funders read tightly-argued answers within their limit as
   more credible than answers that stop short or need trimming live in
   the portal. Draft offline against a copy of the exact fields first,
   never compose inside the portal itself.
2. **Lead the proposed-program answer with the ask and the existing
   grant it complements**, not with organizational background: "requests
   $100,000 to deepen the expansion... In August 2025, the Aim High/NY
   Life Foundation committed $100,000..." A reviewer needs the dollar
   figure and the funding landscape in the first two sentences.
3. **Frame the ask as one layer of a larger, already-funded system, not
   a standalone request.** When another funder already funds part of the
   same initiative, name that funder, the dollar amount, and exactly
   which layer they fund, then define this request as the complementary,
   non-duplicative layer. This does two things at once: it proves the
   program is real and already operating (de-risking the ask) and it
   pre-answers the "other committed funding" question before the portal
   even asks it separately.
4. **Repeat the same three-layer model (here: Clinical / Peer / Family)
   verbatim across multiple answers** (innovation, services-delivered,
   professional-oversight, evaluation). Reusing the same named layers
   with the same labels in every answer, rather than re-describing the
   program differently each time, makes the whole application read as
   one coherent model rather than several loosely related answers.
5. **Name the frameworks and instruments precisely and reuse them across
   answers** (CASEL, Trust-Based Relational Intervention/TBRI, DESSA
   Mini). Precision here substitutes for length; naming the exact
   assessment tool does more evaluative work in a 1500-character field
   than a paragraph of unnamed description would.
6. **Budget answer states the combined multi-funder total first, then
   breaks out only the portion being requested**, itemized to the dollar
   against the exact ask. Never present just this funder's slice without
   first anchoring it inside the full program cost; a reviewer wants to
   see the whole picture and exactly where their money sits in it.
7. **Scale/replication answer ties back to organizational structure**
   (the "community quarterback" GOODZones model), not just program
   mechanics, since "Potential"-type criteria are really asking whether
   the organization, not just this one program, can carry replication.

## Style notes distinct from the standalone-narrative format (contrast with the earlier `.../foundation grant draft` style)

- **No free-standing executive summary or section headers of GOODProjects'
  own choosing.** A portal-driven application has no "structure" beyond
  the funder's own field list; resist the instinct to reintroduce
  canon-narrative section headers (Executive Summary, Community Need,
  Sustainability) where the portal doesn't ask for them.
- **Character counts are tracked and shown inline** (e.g. `*(488/500
  characters)*`) during drafting so nothing needs re-trimming under
  portal pressure at submission time. Carry this practice into future
  portal applications.
- **Verification flags travel with the specific field they affect, not
  in a separate section only**, when a claim is carried over from a
  prior cycle's application (e.g. the 120/90%/80% outcome figures,
  copied from a 2025-cycle submission). A dedicated internal-notes
  section at the end is still useful for anything that must never be
  pasted into the portal, but flag time-sensitive or unverified figures
  right where they'll be used, since that's where a reviewer catches
  them before copying into the live form.
- **No em or en dashes; commas or sentence breaks used instead**,
  consistent with the mandatory humanize pass in `PLAYBOOK.md` Part 8.

## Funder-process facts worth carrying forward (Morgan Stanley Innovation Awards specifically)

- Live submission portal: `morganstanley.theonevalley.com`. (An earlier,
  incorrect assumption pointed to `app-ms.babele.co`; confirm the actual
  portal URL directly from the funder's current cycle materials before
  building any answer set, since it can change between cycles or be
  misremembered from a prior year.)
- Funds are restricted to direct project costs; no institutional
  overhead, per program guidelines.
- The application separately asks about interest in the Leadership
  Learning Series and pro bono Morgan Stanley volunteer support; these
  are low-stakes "Yes" answers worth confirming still reflect current
  organizational bandwidth before reuse.
- Notification is not until spring 2027 for the 2026 cycle; this is a
  seed/pilot-runway award, not fast turnaround funding.
