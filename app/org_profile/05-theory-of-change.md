## Core Conviction

GOODProjects' theory of change starts from a single conviction embedded in our mission: underserved families cannot break cycles of intergenerational poverty through isolated, episodic services alone.

Poverty and violence are not separate problems — they are the same wound. Therefore, our intervention is not a collection of programs but a single, seamless ecosystem of care that surrounds families from every angle: stabilizing the home, developing the child, and interrupting violence on the street.

A child cannot learn when they are hungry or housing-insecure. A parent cannot build wealth when trapped in crisis. And no amount of classroom support matters if the walk home is dangerous. Our integrated continuum closes those gaps.

The desired outcome is not temporary relief but generational transformation: financial independence, educational attainment, and sustained community safety — passed from parent to child, year after year. We do not treat symptoms. We redesign the environment so families can finally thrive.

## The Logic: How the Continuum Works End-to-End

**Illustrative case (based on real participant experience):**

1. **FSP Enrollment:** A single mother of three in Syphax Gardens is referred by her resident council leader. A Family Success Coach helps her secure childcare subsidies, enroll in Medicaid, obtain a new ID, and begin a financial literacy course. Her two school-aged children are enrolled in the GOODLearningHub.

2. **GOODLearningHub:** Her children attend after-school daily. A Youth Services Advocate notices her oldest son (age 10) is withdrawn and struggling with reading. The YSA provides 1:1 tutoring using IXL and coordinates with his teacher. Grades and confidence improve. He joins a weekly peace circle.

3. **GOODZone Intervention:** The mother's nephew (age 19), not yet enrolled, is identified by GOODZone staff as involved in a neighborhood crew conflict. A Credible Messenger — who knows him from the basketball court and whose credibility comes partly from the family's visible trust in GOODProjects — engages him. The conflict is mediated. The young man connects with a Crisis Intervention Specialist and then an FSP Coach, who helps him get his GED, enroll in a SWBID hospitality training program, and open a savings account.

**The key insight:** the trust that made intervention possible at step 3 was built by the visibility of GOODProjects across steps 1 and 2. Each phase activates the next.

## Two-Generation Model

The most distinctive feature of GOODProjects' model is that it operates simultaneously on parents and children. While youth move through JUMPSTART, their parents and caregivers remain enrolled in FSP, continuing to receive financial literacy coaching, employment support, and barrier reduction services.

A stable household is the single most powerful predictor of successful youth employment transitions. The model is not youth-only or family-only — it is two-generation by design.

## Equity Framework (for Grant Narratives)

GOODProjects' equity commitment is not rhetorical — it is structural:

- **Hiring:** Staff and board members are actively recruited from the communities served. People with lived experience of the challenges we address have priority.
- **Program design:** Programs are co-created with community members, not designed for them.
- **Success definition:** Defined by the goals families set for themselves, not by external benchmarks.
- **Barrier elimination:** We do not ask people to overcome barriers; we remove the barriers.
- **Geographic equity:** All three programs operate within walking distance of the public housing communities they serve.
