# GOODSports — Sports Intervention Programme

> Distilled from the "GOODProjects Sports Intervention Programme" concept
> note (donor-agnostic, dated March 2026, contact Neo Mangope). GOODSports
> is the physical-activity/life-skills track within GOODLearningHub (Phase
> II of the continuum) — use this file when a funder specifically asks
> about sports, recreation, physical activity, or life-skills programming
> rather than tutoring/academics broadly.

**Status:** Existing / expansion-ready, operational since 2019. Not a
pilot — this is a proven program seeking catalytic funding to deepen
facilitation quality, not to start from scratch.

**Reach:** 150 youth in summer (GOODCamp, 8 weeks daily) + 50–100 youth in
afterschool (30+ weeks weekly) — consistent with the 150/50–100 figures in
`02-programs.md` and `04-impact-data.md`.

**Population:** Black youth ages 5–14, 100% below the federal poverty
line, living in Southwest DC public housing (Greenleaf community and
surrounding properties).

## What it does

Uses football (soccer), basketball, and dance as intentional vehicles —
not just recreation — to build teamwork, discipline, goal-setting,
conflict resolution, perseverance, and emotional regulation. Coaches and
Youth Services Advocates actively connect the physical activity to life
outcomes rather than simply supervising play.

- **Sports offerings:** football, basketball, dance — each 60 minutes,
  daily in summer / weekly in afterschool.
- **Weekly Life Skills Huddle (20 min):** explicitly links sports
  experience to conflict resolution, goal-setting, emotional regulation,
  and leadership.
- **Academic integration** (not a replacement for academics): High-Impact
  Tutoring (Science of Reading, UFLI Phonics, IXL), personalized math,
  and in-school interventions for 50 at-risk students at partner schools
  (Amidon-Bowen Elementary, Jefferson Middle, +3 more).
- **Family/wraparound supports enabling 95% attendance:** transportation
  (Metro cards, van service), emergency financial assistance, daily meals
  (DC Central Kitchen), and FSP case management.

## Recruitment pathway

1. **FSP:** families already enrolled in wraparound services have youth
   automatically enrolled in GOODSports.
2. **School partnerships:** Amidon-Bowen Elementary, Jefferson Middle, +3
   schools refer students.
3. **Community canvassing:** Resident Council Presidents and GOODZone
   outreach workers identify idle or at-risk youth.

## Metrics and targets

| Metric | Target |
|---|---|
| Reading proficiency improvement (1+ grade level) | 85% of 150 participants |
| Math proficiency improvement (1+ grade level) | 90% of participants |
| Attendance rate | 95% |
| Teamwork/cooperation improvement | 80% of participants |
| Self-regulation/impulse control improvement | 80% of participants |
| Conflict resolution (self-reported) | 75% report increased ability to resolve disagreements calmly |
| Perseverance/task persistence | 70% demonstrate increased persistence |
| Peer leadership | 20 participants become junior coaches/peer leaders |

Long-term goals: participants become community leaders/mentors (3–5
years); reduced violence/justice-system involvement (5+ years); model
scales to other DC wards (2030).

## Readiness checklist (useful for a "capacity to execute" section)

Facilities secured, staff hired, partnership MOUs in place (DPR, DCHA, DC
Central Kitchen, schools), recruitment pipeline active (FSP caseload +
school referrals), curriculum/session plans existing, evaluation
framework in place (Urban Institute + Salesforce). The stated gap is only
staff training, dedicated equipment, and formalizing the life-skills
curriculum — frame any ask as deepening an operating program, not
launching one.
