# SW Peace Garden — Trauma-Informed Programming

> Distilled from "GOODProjects: SW Peace Garden Trauma-Informed
> Programming." Use this file for gardening/food-justice/environmental,
> arts-and-wellness, or trauma-recovery-framed proposals. The Peace Garden
> is also referenced briefly in `02-programs.md` ("Additional Programs")
> and has at least one funded award on record: Progressive Life
> Center/Building Blocks, $5,000, 2022 (`07-awarded-grants-history.md`).

**Core philosophy:** The SW Peace Garden is not framed as an urban farm —
it's positioned as a therapeutic landscape and a proactive, holistic
intervention in the ecosystem of community violence. Trauma is treated as
a public health issue; healing happens through connection — to the earth,
to nourishing food, to community, and to oneself.

## Five program pillars

1. **Therapeutic Horticulture & Somatic Engagement** — structured,
   age-appropriate gardening tasks (planting, watering, harvesting).
   Rationale: grounding work with soil/plants provides sensory
   regulation, reduces cortisol, teaches mindfulness; nature's
   predictable cycles model stability, growth, and resilience.
2. **Social-Emotional Learning (SEL) in Nature's Classroom** — facilitated
   circle time, cooperative projects, conflict-resolution activities tied
   to garden care. Rationale: a neutral, non-clinical space to practice
   emotional vocabulary, self-regulation, empathy; caring for living
   things builds responsibility and self-efficacy.
3. **Nutritional Empowerment & Food Justice Education** — cooking
   demonstrations, "seed-to-table" lessons, food-access discussions.
   Rationale: addresses food apartheid; growing/preparing one's own food
   combats powerlessness and builds sovereign life skills; food framed as
   foundational medicine for body and mind.
4. **Safe Haven & Pro-Social Community Building** — consistent, reliable
   after-school and weekend programming in a welcoming green space.
   Rationale: counters environmental stressors with a physically and
   psychologically safe, predictable sanctuary; fosters peer connection
   and mentorship with trusted adults.
5. **Intergenerational Healing & Family Engagement** — family harvest
   days, shared garden meals, caregiver workshops. Rationale: healing
   must extend to the family unit; creates new, healthy shared narratives
   outside of crisis.

## Target outcomes

- **For youth:** reduced anxiety/stress symptoms; improved emotional
  regulation; enhanced sense of safety and belonging; practical life and
  teamwork skills.
- **For the community:** increased access to fresh, healthy food;
  transformation of vacant lots into hubs of safety and productivity; a
  network of residents engaged in mutual support and collective healing.

**One-line framing for exec summaries:** "The SW Peace Garden uses the
act of growing food as the vehicle to grow people."
