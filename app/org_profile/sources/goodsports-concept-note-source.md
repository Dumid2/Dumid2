# **GOODProjects Sports Intervention Programme**

## **Concept Note / Programme Structure Document**

Programme Name: GOODSports  
Parent Initiative: GOODLearning Hub (GOODProjects, Inc.)  
Location: Southwest D.C. Public Housing Community (Ward 6\)  
Target Population: Black youth ages 5–14 living below the poverty line  
Annual Participant Count: 150 youth (summer) \+ 50–100 youth (afterschool)  
Programme Status: Existing / Expansion-ready (operational since 2019\)

---

## **1\. Programme Overview**

GOODSports is the physical activity and life skills development track within GOODProjects' broader GOODLearning Hub. It uses football (soccer), basketball, and dance as intentional vehicles to build teamwork, discipline, goal-setting, conflict resolution, perseverance, and emotional regulation among youth living in one of Washington, D.C.'s most under-resourced public housing communities.

The programme operates year-round:

* Summer (GOODCamp): 8 weeks, daily sports sessions for 150 youth  
* Afterschool (GOODLearning Hub): 30+ weeks, weekly sports sessions for 50–100 youth

GOODSports is not a standalone recreation programme. It is embedded within a holistic continuum of care that includes high-impact academic tutoring, family success coaching, and violence interruption services.

---

## **2\. Core Problem Statement**

Youth in Southwest D.C. public housing face a convergence of challenges:

| Barrier | Description |
| :---- | :---- |
| Community violence | Persistent gun violence creates chronic hypervigilance and trauma exposure |
| Poverty | 100% of families live below the federal poverty line |
| Food insecurity | High rates of hunger, which exacerbates emotional dysregulation |
| Mental health access | Long waitlists for pediatric therapy; stigma around formal mental health care |
| Academic gaps | Learning loss and below-grade-level proficiency in reading and math |
| Idle time | After-school hours (3:30–6:30 PM) are peak risk hours for violence involvement |

GOODSports directly addresses the mental health, social-emotional, and physical development needs that schools and clinical systems are not meeting at scale.

## **3\. Programme Structure**

### **3.1 Sports Offerings**

| Activity | Frequency | Duration | Mental / Social-Emotional Outcome |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Football (soccer) | Daily (summer) / Weekly (afterschool) | 60 min | Teamwork, stress recovery after failure, perseverance |
| Basketball | Daily (summer) / Weekly (afterschool) | 60 min | Impulse control, discipline, goal-setting |
| Dance | Daily (summer) / Weekly (afterschool) | 60 min | Body awareness, emotional expression, confidence |

All sessions are facilitated, not just supervised. Coaches and Youth Services Advocates actively connect physical activity to life outcomes.

### **3.2 Weekly "Life Skills Huddle"**

In addition to sports sessions, participants attend a weekly 20-minute Life Skills Huddle that explicitly links sports experiences to:

* Conflict resolution (how to de-escalate on the court and in life)  
* Goal-setting (short-term fitness goals → long-term life goals)  
* Emotional regulation (what to do when frustrated, angry, or anxious)  
* Leadership (how to be a teammate, mentor, and community asset)

### **3.3 Integration with Academics**

GOODSports does not replace academics—it complements them. All participants also receive:

| Academic Component | Description | Target Outcome |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| High-Impact Tutoring | 1:1 or small-group tutoring using Science of Reading, UFLI Phonics, and IXL adaptive learning | 85% improve at least one grade level in reading |
| Math instruction | Personalized IXL math sessions | 90% improve at least one grade level in math |
| In-school interventions | For 50 at-risk students at partner schools (e.g., Amidon-Bowen Elementary) | Reduced behavioral incidents; improved classroom engagement |

### **3.4 Family & Wraparound Supports**

To achieve 95% attendance, GOODSports removes barriers through:

* Transportation support (Metro cards, van service)  
* Emergency financial assistance (for families in acute crisis)  
* Daily nutritious meals (via DC Central Kitchen partnership)  
* Family Success Planning (case management for housing, benefits, employment)

## **4\. Target Population & Recruitment**

### **4.1 Demographics**

| Characteristic | Detail |
| :---- | :---- |
| Race/Ethnicity | 100% Black / African American |
| Age range | 5–14 years old |
| Income | 100% below federal poverty line |
| Housing | Public housing (Greenleaf community and surrounding Southwest D.C. properties) |
| Trauma exposure | High rates of witnessed or experienced community violence |

### **4.2 Recruitment Pathway**

Participants are recruited through three channels:

1. Family Success Planning (FSP): Families already enrolled in GOODProjects' wraparound services have their youth automatically enrolled in GOODSports.  
2. School partnerships: Teachers and administrators at Amidon-Bowen Elementary, Jefferson Middle, and three additional schools refer students who would benefit from structured physical activity and mentoring.  
3. Community canvassing: Resident Council Presidents and GOODZone outreach workers identify youth who are idle, disengaged, or at risk of violence involvement.

## **10\. Summary Statement**

GOODSports is a proven, implementable, integrated sports intervention embedded within a decade-old community infrastructure. It serves 150 high-need Black youth annually, using football, basketball, and dance to teach teamwork, discipline, goal-setting, conflict resolution, and perseverance—competencies that translate directly to classroom success, violence prevention, and long-term economic mobility.

The programme is not a theory or a pilot. It operates today. What is needed is modest catalytic funding to deepen the facilitation quality, purchase dedicated equipment, and formalise the life skills curriculum.

With or without external funding, GOODSports will continue. But with targeted investment, it can become a replicable model for how sports-enabled, trauma-informed youth development eliminates barriers and transforms underserved communities.
