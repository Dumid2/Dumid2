### **GOODProjects: SW Peace Garden Trauma-Informed Programming**

**Core Philosophy:** The SW Peace Garden is not just an urban farm; it is a therapeutic landscape and a proactive, holistic intervention in the ecosystem of community violence. We operate on the understanding that trauma is a public health issue and that healing happens through connection—to the earth, to nourishing food, to a supportive community, and to oneself.

**Program Pillars for Youth & Families**:

1. Therapeutic Horticulture & Somatic Engagement:  
   * Activity: Structured, age-appropriate gardening tasks (planting, watering, harvesting).  
   * Trauma-Informed Rationale: Grounding work with soil and plants provides sensory regulation, reduces cortisol levels, and teaches mindfulness. The predictable cycles of nature offer a model for stability, growth, and resilience.  
2. Social-Emotional Learning (SEL) in Nature's Classroom:  
   * Activity: Facilitated "circle time" in the garden, cooperative projects, and conflict-resolution activities tied to garden care.  
   * Trauma-Informed Rationale: The garden is a neutral, non-clinical space to practice emotional vocabulary, self-regulation, empathy, and cooperative skills. Caring for living things builds a sense of responsibility and self-efficacy.  
3. Nutritional Empowerment & Food Justice Education:  
   * Activity: Cooking demonstrations, "seed-to-table" lessons, and discussions on food access.  
   * Trauma-Informed Rationale: Reclaims personal and community health by addressing food apartheid. Learning to grow and prepare one's own food combats powerlessness and builds practical, sovereign life skills. Nutritious food is framed as foundational medicine for the body and mind.  
4. Safe Haven & Pro-Social Community Building:  
   * Activity: Consistent, reliable after-school and weekend programming in a welcoming, green space.  
   * Trauma-Informed Rationale: Counters environmental stressors by providing a physically and psychologically safe, predictable sanctuary. Fosters positive peer connections and mentorship relationships with trusted adults, creating a protective community web.  
5. Intergenerational Healing & Family Engagement:  
   * Activity: Family harvest days, shared meals from the garden, and workshops for caregivers.  
   * Trauma-Informed Rationale: Recognizes that healing must extend to the family unit. Provides a positive, shared activity that strengthens family bonds and creates new, healthy narratives outside of crisis.

**Target Outcomes:**

* For Youth: Reduced symptoms of anxiety and stress; improved emotional regulation; enhanced sense of safety and belonging; development of practical life and teamwork skills.  
* For the Community: Increased access to fresh, healthy food; transformation of vacant lots into hubs of safety and productivity; a cultivated network of residents engaged in mutual support and collective healing.

**In Essence:** The SW Peace Garden uses the act of growing food as the vehicle to grow people. It is a place where youth and families impacted by systemic violence and trauma can find refuge, rebuild a sense of agency, and literally nurture new possibilities for themselves and their community from the ground up.
