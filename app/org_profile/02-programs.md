## The Integrated Continuum of Care

GOODProjects operates an integrated, end-to-end continuum that surrounds families with support at every stage — from the front door of social services to the safe spaces of after-school programming to the street corners where violence disrupts lives. This is not a collection of isolated programs. It is a single, seamless ecosystem of care, refined, tested, and proven in Ward 6 over ten years.

| Phase | Program | What It Does | Who It Serves |
|---|---|---|---|
| I – Stabilize | Family Success Planning (FSP) | Emergency stabilization, benefits navigation, financial literacy, employment support, barrier removal. Every enrolled family receives a dedicated Family Success Coach and a customized Family Success Plan. | Parents & caregivers; youth 14–24 |
| II – Protect | GOODLearning Hub | Afterschool safe harbor: high-dosage tutoring, Youth Services Advocates, Social-Emotional Learning, peace circles, daily family touchpoints. Children of FSP-enrolled families are automatically connected. | Youth ages 5–14 |
| III – Intervene | GOODZone Violence Interruption | Street-level outreach, conflict mediation, rapid crisis response (within 48 hrs of any shooting), custom notifications, and on-the-ground workforce connections via SWBID. Credible Messengers bring the hardest-to-reach into the continuum. | Young adults 18–35; high-risk community members |
| IV – Employ | JUMPSTART / Youth Workforce Development | Alumni tracking, career readiness training, paid internship placement, permanent job transition, and 90-day retention support. | GOODLearningHub alumni ages 14–24 |

## Program 1: Family Success Planning (FSP)

**Lead:** Marquel Webb (FSP Lead)

### What FSP Is
FSP is a collaborative partnership between GOODProjects, local and federal Housing Authorities, grassroots organizations, and service providers. It delivers culturally competent, community-based services within the GOODZone. It is the stabilization foundation and the entry point into GOODProjects' full continuum.

### Target Population
Black families living in Southwest DC's Ward 6, primarily in James Creek, Syphax Gardens, and Greenleaf public housing. Includes victims of crime (gun violence, assault, family violence) and their secondary survivors (children, caregivers, extended family).

### Eligibility
Residents of Southwest DC with active or eligible insurance. Must show proof of residency and Medicaid card at intake.

### Approach: Strengthening Families Protective Factors
Coaches use strengths-based 1:1 coaching across five protective factors:
1. Parental Resilience
2. Social Connections
3. Concrete Support in Times of Need
4. Knowledge of Parenting and Child Development
5. Social and Emotional Competence of Children

### Life Domains Covered
Daily Life & Employment; Community Living; Safety & Security; Healthy Living; Social & Spiritual; Citizenship; Advocacy.

### Core Activities
- Individualized trauma-informed assessments and safety planning (within 2 weeks of intake)
- Crisis intervention and advocacy for families experiencing violence, eviction, or financial crisis
- Resource navigation: mental health services, legal aid, housing support, financial coaching
- Youth programming connection: FSP-enrolled families automatically link to GOODLearningHub
- Southwest Peace Gardens: therapeutic gardening and healing-centered community events

### Data & Tools
- Salesforce CRM (partnered with core service agencies)
- Excel tracking: demographics, child name, phone, email, address, medical number, grade, school, session notes
- Monthly intake reports; internally created client surveys; comparison against modeled Family Success Plan

### FY25 FSP Metrics
- 130 individuals served in FSP
- $100,000+ in direct support distributed to families
- Housing stabilization support provided to 2 families
- 186 community members engaged through violence prevention and trauma response

## Program 2: GOODLearningHub (GLH)

**Lead:** Stephanie Page-Baxter, Director of Youth Development  
**Coordinator:** Phileke Holland (he/him), Youth Program / Afterschool Coordinator

### What the GLH Is
The GOODLearningHub is a year-round academic, health, and social-emotional support program for youth in Southwest DC. It operates at King Greenleaf Recreation Center and serves as the youth-facing anchor of GOODProjects' continuum of care.

### Target Population
Youth ages 5–14 living in Southwest DC public housing. Primarily Black youth living below the poverty line.

### Annual Reach
- 73 students enrolled as of April 2026 (49 boys, 24 girls; ages 6–15)
- 400+ youth served daily (including summer)
- 150 youth in summer (GOODCamps)
- 50–100 youth in afterschool (GOODSports/GLH)

### FY25 GLH Metrics
- 110 in-person program dates
- 11,220 meals served
- 264 tutoring sessions delivered
- 1,313+ hours of active learning and tutoring
- 174,653 questions answered (academic growth)
- 1,912 skills mastered across core subjects

### Core Activities
- High-dosage 1:1 and small-group tutoring (IXL platform)
- Youth Services Advocates (YSAs) — each student assigned a dedicated YSA
- Social-Emotional Learning (SEL): peace circles, conflict resolution, mindfulness, yoga
- Daily nutritious meals
- Mental health services (on-site psychiatric and medical services)
- Parent Cafes: community connection and caregiver support
- GOODSports: football (soccer), basketball, and dance as life-skills vehicles
- GOODCamps: 8-week summer enrichment (tutoring, mentorship, recreation, nutrition)
- STREAM programming (Science, Technology, Reading, Engineering, Arts, Math)

### Key Partnerships
- Amidon-Bowen Elementary School
- Jefferson Middle School (Jefferson Academy)
- DC Central Kitchen (nutrition)
- CityTutor DC
- C3 Wellness (mental health)
- CTDC App (tutoring tracking)

### Awards (FY26)
- FY26 Community Development Grant (CDG) awarded — GOODLearningHub track

## Program 3: GOODZone Violence Interruption

### What It Is
Street-level outreach, conflict mediation, and rapid crisis response operating in Southwest DC's Ward 6. Credible Messengers — trusted community members with lived experience — engage individuals at highest risk and bring them into the continuum.

### Key Data Points
- 45% reduction in violent crime in PSA 105 (2023–2024), compared to a 12% increase citywide — validated by independent MPD data analysis
- Over six years: GOODZone recorded only 249 violent incidents vs. 601 in neighboring Navy Yard
- 186 community members engaged in violence prevention and trauma response (FY25)
- Crisis response within 48 hours of any shooting

### Key Partners
- DC Office of Neighborhood Safety and Engagement (ONSE)
- DC Department of Behavioral Health (DBH)
- OVSJG Crime Victims Program
- Southwest Business Improvement District (SWBID)
- Metropolitan Police Department (data validation)

## Program 4: JUMPSTART — Youth Workforce Development Initiative

**Also known as:** "JUMPSTART Pathways to Income" in workforce/employer-facing contexts.

### What It Is
A four-stage alumni employment pathway that converts nine years of investment in GOODLearningHub youth into permanent employment. It does not create new programs for new people — it formalizes existing work: pulling alumni back as mentors, interns, and employees, while connecting them to GOODZone Business Partners ready to hire.

### Target Population
GOODLearningHub alumni ages 14–24, living in Ward 6, with an active FSP family connection. At risk of idling and economic exclusion post-program.

### The Four-Stage Pathway

| Stage | Component | What Happens | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Alumni Tracking & Intake | Proactive outreach; intake assessment; dedicated coach assigned | Weeks 1–2 |
| 2 | Career Launchpad Training | 40-hour intensive: resume, interview, professional communication, financial literacy, SEL, workplace etiquette | Weeks 3–4 |
| 3 | Paid Internship Placement | Direct placement with GOODZone Business Partner; $15/hour, 20 hrs/week, 6 weeks; weekly trauma-informed wellness check-ins | Weeks 5–10 |
| 4 | Placement & 90-Day Retention | Dedicated Placement Coordinator; transition from internship to permanent hire; retention tracking | Weeks 11–24 |

### Proven Track Record
- 50+ youth in paid/volunteer peer mentorship roles (2021–present)
- 100+ alumni engaged with zero incidents of violence or program disruption
- Nasdaq Women's Financial Literacy Program (2025): 15 completers, 2 launched registered businesses in Ward 6
- SWBID: 5 youth annually in paid stipend positions within GOODProjects team
- D.O.E.S. active partnership

## Program 5: Black Justice Fund (BJF)

### What It Is
GOODProjects' systems-change and civic leadership arm. Equips Black leaders from across the DMV to pilot grassroots economic innovations. Funded in 2025 with a $600,000 commitment from the Robert Wood Johnson Foundation.

### Current Focus (2025–2026)
- Community-led justice initiatives
- Youth civic leadership development
- Narrative change work around Black communities
- Purpose Tour 2025: 10-city national series for community leaders (launched in response to federal DEI rollbacks)
- Black Justice Fund (2025): initial $150,000 commitment, supporting 8–10 grassroots leaders nationally
