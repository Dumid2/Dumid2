**Mission (500 char max):** GOODProjects is a Washington, DC nonprofit
working to end the cycle of intergenerational poverty in Southwest DC
through a two-generation, place-based model. We pair children's
education and youth development with the economic mobility, food
security, health, and stability of the adults around them, so progress
in one generation reinforces the next. Poverty and violence in our
community are inseparable from mental health, so trauma-informed care
runs through every program we operate. *(488/500 characters)*

**Primary programs and services (1000 char max):** GOODProjects operates
a four-phase continuum in Southwest DC's Ward 6 public housing
communities. Family Success Planning stabilizes families: each gets a
dedicated coach for financial coaching, benefits navigation, and
workforce readiness, serving 130 individuals and distributing $100,000+
in FY25. GOODLearningHub protects and develops youth ages 5 to 18
through high-dosage tutoring, daily meals, and social-emotional
learning, plus, since August 2025, licensed clinical mental health
services, reaching 400+ youth daily and delivering 1,313+ tutoring hours
in FY25. GOODZone intervenes through Credible Messengers' daily
street-level outreach and crisis response within 48 hours of any
shooting, driving a 45% drop in violent crime against a 12% citywide
rise. JUMPSTART employs GOODLearningHub alumni ages 14 to 24 through
career training, paid internships, and 90-day retention support. The SW
Peace Garden, launched 2021, extends healing through trauma-informed
therapeutic horticulture. *(995/1000 characters)*

**Primary population served (2500 char max):** We serve residents of
James Creek, Syphax Gardens, and Greenleaf, three public housing
communities in Southwest DC's Ward 6, where GOODProjects has operated
continuously for 10 years. Approximately 95% of participants are Black
or African American, and median household income across our community
is approximately $14,000 a year, a fraction of the $80,000 threshold
GOODProjects works to help families reach and well below DC's citywide
median.

Ward 6 saw a 25% year-over-year increase in gun-related incidents in
2024 (MPD), even as GOODProjects' own GOODZone footprint (PSA 105)
recorded a 45% reduction in violent crime against a 12% citywide
increase over the same period, a gap that reflects the effect of
sustained, place-based intervention rather than an absence of risk.
Families here face compounding challenges: generational poverty,
housing insecurity, unemployment, and chronic exposure to community
violence and trauma, layered on top of the systemic disinvestment that
has historically left Southwest DC's public housing communities
under-resourced relative to wealthier DC neighborhoods.

This request specifically targets youth within GOODZone's PSA 105
footprint who are exposed, directly or as bystanders, to a
community-violence stressor event: a shooting, a death, or another
crisis event in their housing community. GOOD Debrief reaches them
through GOODZone's existing violence-interruption network, the same
Credible Messengers and Crisis Intervention Specialists already present
within 48 hours of any shooting, rather than through a separate intake
process. Because the tool is triggered by a specific event rather than a
fixed age band, it reaches whichever youth were present for or affected
by that event, spanning the range of children and teens GOODZone already
serves. Their families, many already known to GOODProjects through
Family Success Planning, are the secondary audience: caregivers whose
children experience a check-in are informed and supported by C3
Wellness's clinical escalation pathway when risk is flagged. *(2055/2500
characters)*

## PROPOSED PROGRAM INFORMATION

**Describe your proposed program (2500 char max):** GOODProjects
requests $100,000 to pilot GOOD Debrief, an AI-assisted, post-stressor
listening tool that gives youth in Southwest DC's James Creek, Syphax
Gardens, and Greenleaf public housing communities an immediate,
low-barrier space to process fear, grief, or distress after exposure to
community violence or other acute stressors, with a built-in clinical
escalation pathway to licensed C3 Wellness providers when a child's
responses signal elevated risk.

GOOD Debrief closes a specific, urgent gap: children in our GOODZone
footprint are repeatedly exposed to trauma-inducing events, but the
traditional referral-to-therapy pipeline is too slow, too stigmatized,
and too disconnected from where kids actually are in the hours and days
after something happens. GOODProjects' GOODZone violence interrupters
and Family Success Coaches are already present at the exact moments of
acute stress that put children at risk, having driven an MPD-validated
45% reduction in violent crime in PSA 105 against a 12% citywide
increase. Today we have no fast, dignified way to check in on a child's
internal state before the next crisis compounds it.

GOOD Debrief is a three-layer model. GOODZone's violence interruption
network flags a qualifying stressor event in real time. Youth then
receive a private, low-barrier AI check-in prompt within 24 to 48 hours.
Responses indicating elevated risk route immediately to a licensed C3
Wellness clinician for human follow-up and care coordination. This grant
funds the runway to build, pilot, and rigorously evaluate that bridge.
*(1566/2500 characters)*

**Problem or unmet need (1000 char max):** Most children's mental health
innovation addresses the system: more clinicians, more school programs,
more awareness campaigns. GOOD Debrief addresses the moment: the narrow,
high-stakes window right after a child in a high-violence-exposure
community experiences something traumatic, when adults are focused on
immediate safety and the child's internal state goes unseen. Kids in
these communities under-report distress not because they aren't
struggling, but because stigma and access friction get in the way, and
the traditional referral-to-therapy pipeline is too slow to meet them in
that window. By pairing AI's ability to be present at scale and without
stigma with C3 Wellness's clinical judgment where it matters most, GOOD
Debrief turns our existing violence-interruption infrastructure, which
already knows when something happened, into infrastructure that also
asks how the child is doing. *(901/1000 characters)*

**New, piloted, or existing?** New. GOOD Debrief has not yet launched;
this grant funds the build, pilot, and evaluation of a new AI-assisted
check-in tool layered onto GOODProjects' existing, already-operating
GOODZone violence-interruption network and C3 Wellness clinical
partnership.

**Innovative aspects; awareness of similar programs; how you distinguish
(1500 char max):** GOOD Debrief is not a general-purpose wellness
chatbot. It is a purpose-built, event-triggered check-in, deployed in
the immediate aftermath of a documented community stressor (a shooting,
a death, a crisis event in the housing community), that gives a child a
private, judgment-free space to name what they're feeling in their own
words, in real time, without first clearing the hurdle of asking an
adult for help. This addresses a barrier our own data surfaces: kids in
high-violence-exposure communities under-report distress not because
they aren't struggling, but because stigma and access friction get in
the way. GOODZone's Credible Messengers and Crisis Intervention
Specialists are already present at the moment a stressor occurs,
infrastructure most youth mental health tools lack and cannot replicate
quickly. We are not aware of another DC violence-interruption
organization pairing real-time crisis flagging with an AI-assisted,
clinically-escalated youth check-in. *(978/1500 characters)*

**Services delivered and how (1500 char max):** GOOD Debrief operates as
a three-layer model. Layer 1, Trigger: GOODZone's violence interrupters
and Family Success Coaches, already embedded in PSA 105, flag qualifying
stressor events in real time. Layer 2, AI Check-In: youth receive a
private, low-barrier prompt to check in on their emotional state within
24 to 48 hours of a flagged event, through the GOOD Debrief conversational
tool. Layer 3, Clinical Escalation: responses indicating elevated risk
route immediately to a licensed C3 Wellness provider for human follow-up
and care coordination. The AI layer is a front door, not a replacement
for care: every elevated-risk interaction routes to a credentialed
clinician within a defined response window. All three layers connect
back to GOODProjects' existing GOODZone community presence, so youth
encounter GOOD Debrief through people they already know and trust, not a
stand-alone app. *(894/1500 characters)*

**How informed/delivered by mental health professionals (1500 char
max):** GOOD Debrief is co-designed with C3 Wellness's licensed
psychosocial care team, GOODProjects' longstanding clinical partner, who
define the escalation triggers, review flagged conversations, and
provide the human clinical response when a child's language indicates
risk. The AI layer is a front door, not a replacement for care; every
elevated-risk interaction routes to a credentialed clinician within a
defined response window. C3 Wellness already provides trauma-informed
therapy and wellness check-ins across GOODProjects' broader continuum,
so GOOD Debrief extends an existing clinical relationship rather than
introducing an untested one. GOODZone staff, trained in trauma-informed
care, are the frontline layer who introduce the tool to a youth in the
moment, guided by C3 Wellness on how to do so without re-traumatizing a
child who has just experienced a stressor event. *(879/1500 characters)*

**Beneficiaries, geography, timeframe (1000 char max):** Over the
12-month grant period, GOOD Debrief will reach youth and families
connected to community-violence stressor events across James Creek,
Syphax Gardens, and Greenleaf, the Southwest DC public housing
communities GOODZone serves within PSA 105. GOODZone responded to 29
violent incidents in this footprint in 2024 alone (MPD-validated), each
a potential trigger event for a GOOD Debrief check-in. As a new pilot,
GOOD Debrief will establish exact youth-reached and escalation-rate
targets during year one as clinical thresholds are calibrated with C3
Wellness, rather than committing to a placeholder count before the tool
exists. The pilot runs the full 12-month grant period through a
complete evaluation cycle. *(718/1000 characters)*

**Measurement and evaluation (1500 char max):** We will track youth
reached per stressor event, time from event to first check-in,
escalation rate to C3 Wellness, and pre/post validated measures of
trauma symptoms and perceived access to support, collected with C3
Wellness's existing evaluation protocols. Our learning cycle runs in
three steps. Gather: de-identified check-in transcripts, escalation
logs, and C3 Wellness clinical notes, alongside youth and caregiver
feedback. Understand: weekly review with C3 Wellness clinicians to
refine escalation thresholds and flag false positives or negatives,
ensuring the tool catches real risk without over-triggering. Apply:
insights drive real-time refinement of GOOD Debrief's escalation logic,
updated staff training for GOODZone violence interrupters and Family
Success Coaches, and a practitioner brief for the community violence
intervention field on AI-assisted, trauma-responsive check-ins. *(898/1500
characters)*

**Feasibility of scale/replication (1500 char max):** Yes. GOOD Debrief
is built to be model-agnostic to place. The trigger-and-escalate
architecture, community event to AI check-in to clinical escalation
protocol, is directly replicable by any direct-service organization with
an on-the-ground presence and a clinical partner, which describes most
community violence intervention (CVI) organizations nationally.
GOODProjects is structured as a "community quarterback" model (the
GOODZones Initiative), coordinating multiple partner organizations
across housing, education, and youth development in one place-based
footprint, so extending GOOD Debrief to additional GOODZone corridors
does not require new organizational infrastructure. A successful DC
pilot gives us a field-ready model, documented through the practitioner
brief, to share with peer CVI and youth-serving organizations
nationally. *(844/1500 characters)*
