## 1. Project Title

Step Into Heritage: A Dance and Humanities Field Trip Series
(10/10 words)

## 2. Executive Summary

GOODProjects requests $7,900 from the DC Commission on the Arts and Humanities to produce a two-part dance and humanities field trip series for 6th-8th grade youth enrolled in GOODLearningHub, GOODProjects' afterschool program serving Southwest DC's Ward 6 public housing communities.

The series pairs a weekday matinee of Step Afrika!'s Magical Musical Holiday Step Show at Arena Stage with a historic site visit to Ford's Theatre through its Free Field Trip Program, connecting the African American stepping tradition to the American history explored at Ford's Theatre. Each trip is paired with a pre-trip classroom workshop and a post-trip reflection session tied to GOODLearningHub's existing Social-Emotional Learning curriculum, and the series concludes with a professional development session for Youth Services Advocates covering both dance-based and history-based instructional integration.

GOODProjects is the producer of this field trip series: designing the curriculum connections, coordinating logistics, leading the pre- and post-trip learning, and evaluating outcomes, while Arena Stage and Step Afrika! produce the Trip 1 performance and Ford's Theatre's education staff lead the Trip 2 site visit.

## 3. Organizational Background

GOODProjects is a Washington, DC-based 501(c)(3) nonprofit founded in February 2016 by Darius Baxter, with programming roots dating to a 2015 summer camp response to a spike in gun violence in Ward 8. Our mission is to empower underserved families to break cycles of intergenerational poverty through integrated, place-based support.

Since 2016, GOODProjects has directed more than $8 million in community investment, primarily in Southwest DC's Ward 6, specifically the James Creek, Syphax Gardens, and Greenleaf public housing communities, anchored at the King Greenleaf Recreation Center.

GOODProjects' year-round afterschool program, GOODLearningHub, is the youth-facing anchor of this work: 73 students enrolled as of April 2026, ages 5-14, primarily Black youth living below the federal poverty line. GOODLearningHub already integrates arts and creative expression into its model: GOODSports includes dance as a life-skills vehicle, and its STREAM programming (Science, Technology, Reading, Engineering, Arts, Math) treats the arts as a core, not supplemental, learning domain.

GOODLearningHub partners directly with Amidon-Bowen Elementary School and Jefferson Middle School (Jefferson Academy): the students who would participate in this field trip series' 6th-8th grade band are GOODLearningHub youth attending Jefferson Middle School during the school day, giving this project a direct, existing connection to DC Public Schools personnel and curriculum, as the RFA's program goals call for. GOODProjects is not itself a dance company, theater company, or historic site, and this narrative does not claim to be. GOODProjects' role is as the producer and organizer of the field trip series: designing the curriculum connections, managing logistics and participation, and evaluating outcomes, while partnering with two established, professional DC institutions, Arena Stage and Ford's Theatre, to deliver the performance, historic site, and teaching-staff components. This is precisely the "presenting organization" model the FTE grant is designed to fund.

## 4. Statement of Need

Students in Southwest DC's Ward 6 public housing communities face real barriers to arts and humanities enrichment: cost, transportation, and limited school capacity for field trips that connect classroom learning to live, professional artistic and historical experience. GOODLearningHub's 73 enrolled students are ages 5-14, approximately 95% Black or African American, and live below 200% of the federal poverty line, a population with meaningfully less access to DC's cultural institutions than their peers in less under-resourced neighborhoods, despite living minutes from both Arena Stage and Ford's Theatre.

A single field trip gives students one exposure to one tradition. A series gives students the chance to build a connected understanding across two: the living, performed cultural tradition of stepping, rooted in HBCU history and Black community life, and the documented, place-based history explored at Ford's Theatre, rooted in the same American story these students are taught in DCPS classrooms. For GOODLearningHub's 6th-8th graders, an age band navigating identity, belonging, and self-expression, connecting a performed tradition and a historic site within the same program year builds a fuller picture of how culture and history intersect than either trip alone.

This project addresses that need directly: two no-cost or largely no-cost, fully-resourced field trip experiences, each paired with real classroom instruction before and after, for a population that is geographically close to both institutions and structurally distant from the opportunity to attend either.

## 5. Project Description

This is a two-trip field trip series delivered across GOODLearningHub's Fall II, Winter, and Spring program sessions, inside a project period beginning after the required October 1, 2026 start date.

**Trip 1: Arena Stage, Step Afrika!'s Magical Musical Holiday Step Show.** Arena Stage's annual family production celebrating the African American stepping tradition, running December 4-23, 2026 at Arena Stage (1101 6th Street SW, Washington, DC), scheduled inside GOODLearningHub's existing Fall II program session (Nov 30 to Dec 17, 2026).

- Pre-Trip Workshop: GOODLearningHub staff lead a classroom session introducing the history and cultural significance of stepping, connecting the production's themes to GOODLearningHub's existing Social-Emotional Learning curriculum: identity, community, and collective voice.
- Field Trip: A weekday matinee performance for 30 GOODLearningHub youth in the 6th-8th grade band, including round-trip transportation.
- On-Site Learning Component: An Arena Stage "Interactive" add-on package: a workshop or backstage tour led by Arena Stage teaching artists, plus a pre-show program and printed Learning Guides for each student.
- Post-Trip Reflection: A GOODLearningHub-led classroom session where students connect the performance to a personal or community reflection.

**Trip 2: Ford's Theatre, Free Field Trip Program (historic site visit).** Ford's Theatre (511 10th Street NW, Washington, DC) offers a Free Field Trip Program to DC Public Schools, DC Chartered Public Schools, and Title I or 40%-plus-FARMs-eligible schools, with free bus transportation available to schools within the Capital Beltway. Proposed for GOODLearningHub's Winter or Spring session (January to June 2027), giving planning time to confirm eligibility and booking after Trip 1.

- Pre-Trip Workshop: GOODLearningHub staff lead a classroom session introducing the history of Lincoln's presidency, the Civil War, and the events at Ford's Theatre, connecting the site's history to GOODLearningHub's existing SEL curriculum themes of leadership, legacy, and community impact.
- Field Trip: A guided visit for the same 30-student cohort through the Ford's Theatre Museum, the theatre itself and President's Box, and the Petersen House and Aftermath Exhibits, led by Ford's Theatre education staff.
- Post-Trip Reflection: A GOODLearningHub-led classroom session where students connect what they learned to a personal or community reflection on leadership and legacy.

**Staff Professional Development:** A session for GOODLearningHub's Youth Services Advocates covering both dance- and movement-based learning and historical-inquiry-based learning, on integrating each into after-school programming, extending the value of this series beyond the two field trips themselves.

## 6. Artistic and Humanities Merit

This series' merit rests on pairing two genuinely high-quality, professionally produced experiences, one artistic and one historical, each with real instructional design built around it, not just attendance.

Stepping is a serious art form: rhythmic, percussive, rooted in African diasporic and HBCU tradition. Step Afrika! is an internationally recognized dance company, and Trip 1 treats the performance as a serious artistic and educational experience for students.

Ford's Theatre is a recognized humanities institution: the National Park Service and Ford's Theatre Society jointly interpret the site of Lincoln's assassination and its aftermath through primary sources and its own education department. Trip 2 gives students direct, place-based engagement with a documented moment in American history, taught by Ford's Theatre's own education staff.

The humanities content connecting both trips is built by GOODLearningHub staff with direct, relevant qualifications: Stephanie Page-Baxter, Director of Youth Programming, holds a B.A. in Education from Hampton University and an M.A. in Curriculum and Instruction from George Washington University, with 34 years as a DCPS educator before joining GOODProjects. She designs the curriculum connections between both experiences and GOODLearningHub's existing Social-Emotional Learning framework. Phileke Holland, Afterschool Lead, leads day-to-day facilitation and has five years of direct experience with GOODLearningHub's student population. Arena Stage's own teaching artists lead the Trip 1 on-site interactive component, and Ford's Theatre's own education staff lead the Trip 2 site visit, each bringing professional education practice directly into the experience.

The pre- and post-trip workshops ensure both field trips are not passive: students engage with each subject's history and significance before attending, and are asked to reflect on and connect it to their own identity, community, or understanding of leadership afterward, developmentally appropriate content for a 6th-8th grade audience navigating those same questions in their own lives.

## 7. Community Access and Engagement

This series is built for a specific, named population, not a general DC audience: 30 youth drawn from GOODLearningHub's 73 enrolled students, residents of the James Creek, Syphax Gardens, and Greenleaf public housing communities in Ward 6, ages 5-14 overall (6th-8th graders specifically for this series), approximately 95% Black or African American, and below 200% of the federal poverty line.

Access is built into the design:

- No cost to families for either trip: Trip 1's full cost is covered by this grant, and Trip 2 is free through Ford's Theatre's own Free Field Trip Program for qualifying schools, pending confirmation of GOODProjects' eligibility.
- Transportation provided: Trip 1's round-trip van/bus transportation is budgeted directly; Trip 2 may qualify for Ford's Theatre's free bus program for schools within the Capital Beltway, which would remove this cost entirely, pending confirmation.
- ADA accessibility: both Arena Stage and Ford's Theatre are fully accessible professional venues.
- Culturally responsive and historically grounded content: Trip 1 reflects the students' own cultural heritage; Trip 2 connects them directly to a documented site in the American history taught in their own DCPS classrooms.
- Existing school partnership: GOODLearningHub's direct partnership with Jefferson Middle School means this series reaches students through an existing, trusted relationship rather than a cold outreach effort.

## 9. Project Goals and Objectives

**Goal 1: Increase access to professional dance and humanities learning.**
Objective: Deliver two fully-funded (or largely no-cost) field trips to 30 GOODLearningHub youth in the 6th-8th grade band, at no cost to families.

**Goal 2: Connect artistic experience and historical understanding to identity and community.**
Objective: Deliver pre- and post-trip workshops for each trip that connect stepping's cultural history and Ford's Theatre's American history to students' own identities, using GOODLearningHub's existing SEL framework.

**Goal 3: Build staff capacity to extend both arts- and humanities-based learning.**
Objective: Deliver a professional development session for GOODLearningHub's Youth Services Advocates on integrating dance-, movement-, and history-based learning into ongoing afterschool programming.

**Goal 4: Strengthen the DCPS/DCPCS connection.**
Objective: Use this project's existing partnership with Jefferson Middle School to ground both field trips in real school-day curriculum connections, not just afterschool enrichment in isolation.

**Goal 5: Establish a repeatable, multi-site field trip model.**
Objective: Use this series as a template GOODLearningHub can repeat in future program years, drawing on the same logistics capacity it already applies to its broader annual field trip calendar.
