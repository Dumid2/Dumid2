## PART ONE: THE NARRATIVE

### 1. Project Title

Rhythm and Roots: An African American History and Arts Field Trip Series
(portal field, 10-word limit: "GOODLearningHub African American Arts and
Humanities Field Trip Series")

### 2. Executive Summary

GOODProjects requests grant funding to provide GOODLearningHub youth in grades
6 through 8 with a themed, one-trip-per-term field trip series across the FY27
grant period (October 1, 2026 through September 30, 2027): African American
history, culture, and artistic tradition as expressions of identity and
community. The anchor trip, Step Afrika's Magical Musical Holiday Step Show at
Arena Stage, establishes the throughline: stepping originated at HBCUs as a
form of cultural storytelling and communal pride. Each of the four
supplementary trips extends that thread into a different art form, era, or DC
community institution, tracing African American artistic tradition from its
roots in enslaved community life (Anacostia Community Museum), through visual
representation of Black identity in American history (National Portrait
Gallery), through DC's own homegrown musical tradition (Go-Go Museum and
Cafe), to the living performance traditions of the African diaspora (Dance
Place's DanceAfrica DC). GOODProjects is not itself a dance company, a
theater, or a museum; it is the presenting and producing organization bringing
its own enrolled youth into direct, recurring contact with DC's arts and
humanities institutions across a full grant year, exactly the model the FTE
grant is built to fund.

### 3. Organizational Background

GOODProjects, Inc. is a Washington, DC nonprofit, founded in 2016 and based at
King Greenleaf Recreation Center in Southwest DC's Ward 6. It operates an
integrated, two-generation continuum of care: Family Success Planning
stabilizes parents and caregivers, GOODLearningHub provides year-round
afterschool and summer enrichment for youth ages 5 to 14, GOODZone interrupts
community violence, and JUMPSTART moves alumni into paid employment. This
request draws on GOODLearningHub, which currently enrolls 73 students and
serves more than 400 youth daily across its full program mix.

### 4. Community Need and Alignment with FTE Priorities

Southwest DC saw a 25 percent increase in gun-related incidents in 2024, and
the families GOODProjects serves face compounding barriers of generational
poverty, housing insecurity, and chronic trauma exposure. Arts and cultural
access is not something these families' schools or neighborhoods otherwise
provide at the scale DC's theaters and Smithsonian institutions can. The FTE
grant requires all funded activity to take place in DC and requires the
applicant to function as the producer of the experience rather than as an
arts organization itself; every venue in this proposal is inside DC for
exactly that reason. GOODProjects already runs a large, real field trip
program (see Section 10, Past Performance), but that existing calendar is
almost entirely outside DC, which is precisely the gap this grant closes: a
DC-based, arts-and-humanities-specific complement to programming GOODProjects
already knows how to plan, staff, and deliver safely at scale.

### 5. Target Population

30 GOODLearningHub youth, grades 6 through 8 (ages approximately 11 to 14),
drawn from James Creek, Syphax Gardens, and Greenleaf public housing in
Southwest DC's Ward 6. Approximately 95 percent Black or African American,
below 200 percent of the federal poverty line. The same 30-youth cohort
attends every trip in the series, so the unduplicated participant count is
30, not a multiple of the trip count.

### 6. Program Design: The Field Trip Series

**Anchor, Winter term, December 2026: Step Afrika's Magical Musical Holiday
Step Show, Arena Stage.**
Fichandler Stage, Arena Stage, 1101 6th St SW, Washington, DC 20024 (Ward 6).
Runs December 4 to 23, 2026; book the matinee anywhere in that window except
December 17, 2026, which is the last day of GOODLearningHub's Fall II session
and the date "Winter City Lights" is already booked on GOODProjects' own
recreational calendar for part of this cohort. Approximately 90 minutes, no
intermission, now in its fifth year at Arena Stage. Arena Stage offers three
tiered educational add-ons for student groups, each requiring a minimum of 30
students: Enriched ($5/student, pre-show program plus printed learning
guides), Interactive ($15/student, adds a workshop or backstage tour), and
Extended Day ($20/student, adds a take-home learning guide). Digital learning
guides are included with any group booking. Group Sales (202-488-4380,
groups@arenastage.org) offers up to 50 percent off standard pricing for
groups of 10 or more; Arena Stage's separate DC Ticket Partnership offers
$4/ticket plus in-class workshops, study guides, and post-show talkbacks for
DCPS/DCPCS schools, with an application typically due in late September.
GOODLearningHub partners with Amidon-Bowen Elementary and Jefferson Middle,
both DCPS schools; whether that makes GOODLearningHub itself eligible for the
DC Ticket Partnership, versus only the general group rate, is an open
question below. Arena Stage is fully ADA accessible, with audio-described and
ASL-interpreted performances scheduled for some productions.

**Trip 1, Fall term, October to December 2026: Anacostia Community Museum,
"We Make History."**
1901 Fort Place SE, Washington, DC 20020 (Ward 8; Anacostia Metro, Green
Line, then the W2/W3 bus or a short rideshare). Free admission (Smithsonian
institution). The exhibition opened May 30, 2026 and runs through January
2028, so any date in the grant period works. Its centerpiece is the diary of
Adam Francis Plummer, described as the only known example of a
multi-generational diary started by a person who was enslaved in the United
States; other holdings include a fur coat worn by Marian Anderson at her 1939
Lincoln Memorial concert and a guitar that belonged to Go-Go Godfather Chuck
Brown. Three PK-12 school programs are listed on the museum's tours page,
two of which fit grades 6 through 8 directly: Visionaries in Art: Exploring
Black Creativity and Expression (grades 3-6, 75 minutes) and Express
Yourself: Art and Identity in Your Community (grades 7-12, 75 minutes,
students create artwork from personal portraits/photos); DC History
1900-2000: A Century of Black Arts Education (grades 5-12, 60 minutes) is
also a strong fit. Groups of 10 or more may request free bus transportation,
first come first served, at least two weeks ahead. A free self-guided
booklet for grades 5-12 is available at the exhibition entrance. CURRENCY
FLAG: as of this research, the museum's public tours page still lists
programs framed around the prior exhibition ("Bold and Beautiful Vision");
confirm directly with the education team whether the three programs above
have been updated for We Make History, or whether new ones exist.

**Trip 2, Winter/Spring term, January to March 2027: Smithsonian National
Portrait Gallery.**
8th and F St NW, Washington, DC 20001 (Ward 2; Gallery Place-Chinatown
Metro, Red/Yellow/Green Lines). Free admission and free student programs.
In-person programs run Tuesday through Friday, October 1, 2026 through May
14, 2027, at 9:30 a.m., 11:00 a.m., and 12:45 p.m., 75 minutes, led by
trained gallery educators, standards-based, maximum 60 students per visit.
Relevant permanent galleries and exhibitions include Taking the Stage (how
African Americans transformed theatrical representation, a direct echo of
the anchor trip's stepping tradition), A Changing America: 1968 and Beyond,
and Reckoning: Protest. Defiance. Resilience. 2026-27 registration opens
August 1, 2026, and requires at least one month's advance notice; specify
grades 6-8 and interest in African American history/culture when
registering so the gallery educator selects the right themed tour. The
Gallery is ADA accessible, with ASL interpretation and tours adapted for
visual impairments and developmental disabilities available on request.
Transportation is not provided by the museum.

**Trip 3, Spring term, February to May 2027: Go-Go Museum and Cafe.**
1920 Martin Luther King Jr Ave SE, Washington, DC 20020 (Ward 8; 0.56 miles
from Anacostia Metro, Green Line; motor coach/bus parking on site). Opened
February 19, 2025, as the first institution dedicated to preserving go-go
music, DC's own homegrown genre, created by Chuck Brown from roots in funk,
hip-hop, and African percussion tradition and now protected as the District's
official music genre. 16 or more digital interactive installations, AI
holograms of go-go musicians, a recording studio, an outdoor stage, and a
monthly Peer to Peer Youth Concert Series bringing young go-go performers in
front of youth audiences. Self-guided tours for groups under 10 are free for
DC residents with ID ($15 for non-residents); guided group tours (10 to 50
people) are reported at approximately $20/person, though that figure traces
to a community post from early 2025, not the museum directly, and should be
reconfirmed. Listed as ADA compliant. Transportation is not provided.

**Trip 4, Summer term, June 2027: Dance Place, DanceAfrica DC.**
3225 8th St NE, Washington, DC 20017 (Ward 5; Brookland-CUA Metro, Red
Line). DanceAfrica DC is Dance Place's annual festival of African diaspora
dance and music, curated in 2026 by Griot Mama Sylvia Soumah of Coyaba Dance
Theater (a resident company specializing in dance and music from Guinea,
Mali, and Senegal, among other nations); it traces the African roots of the
same movement traditions that shape the anchor trip's stepping content. The
2026 festival ran June 1 to 7, 2026; 2027 dates were not announced as of this
research, though the festival has run every year without interruption.
Historical Family Series pricing let children 12 and under attend free with
a paying adult, and student tickets have run around $15. Contact Dance
Place's education team (202-269-1600, shannonq@danceplace.org) about a
school group rate, a youth matinee, or a pre/post-show teaching-artist
workshop once 2027 dates are set.

Every trip is preceded by a pre-trip classroom workshop connecting that
venue's content to GOODLearningHub's existing social-emotional learning
curriculum, and followed by a post-trip workshop. The anchor trip
additionally includes a staff professional development session and the
Interactive add-on described above.

**Additional venues identified, not yet built into the budget below:** Ford's
Theatre's Legacy Commissions "A First Look" festival has, in past years,
included work centered on African American history (a January 2026 reading of
*Young John Lewis: Prodigy of Protest*); its January 2027 program is not yet
announced. Ford's Theatre offers a free field trip program, including free
bus transportation within the Capital Beltway, for qualifying DCPS/DCPCS/
Title I schools. THEARC (Town Hall Education Arts Recreation Campus) hosts
Phillips@THEARC, offering free field trips and arts-integration partnerships
with Ward 7 and 8 schools, and an annual "Blacks in Wax" youth performance
typically held in March. Howard Theatre is a historically significant Black
DC venue with no confirmed school-group program found in this research.
These three are worth direct outreach as stretch additions or backups, given
the budget headroom below, but none is committed in this draft.
