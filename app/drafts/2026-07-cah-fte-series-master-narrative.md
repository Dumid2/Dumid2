## PART ONE: THE NARRATIVE

### 1. Project Title

Rhythm and Roots: An African American History and Arts Field Trip Series
(portal field, 10-word limit: "GOODLearningHub African American Arts and
Humanities Field Trip Series")

### 2. Executive Summary

GOODProjects requests grant funding to provide GOODLearningHub youth in grades
6 through 8 with a themed series of field trips across the FY27 grant period
(October 1, 2026 through September 30, 2027), unified around African American
history, culture, and artistic tradition, timed to coincide with the national
250th anniversary commemoration that several of the confirmed venues are
themselves programming around. GOODProjects is not itself a dance company, a
theater, or a museum. It is the presenting and producing organization bringing
its own enrolled youth into direct contact with DC's arts and humanities
institutions, exactly the model the FTE grant is built to fund. The series
anchors on two confirmed Step Afrika productions at Arena Stage, and adds two
confirmed Smithsonian venues with programming that runs the length of the
grant period, giving the cohort recurring, structured arts and humanities
contact across four seasons rather than a single one-off outing.

### 3. Organizational Background

GOODProjects, Inc. is a Washington, DC nonprofit, founded in 2016 and based at
King Greenleaf Recreation Center in Southwest DC's Ward 6. It operates an
integrated, two-generation continuum of care: Family Success Planning
stabilizes parents and caregivers, GOODLearningHub provides year-round
afterschool and summer enrichment for youth ages 5 to 14, GOODZone interrupts
community violence, and JUMPSTART moves alumni into paid employment. This
request draws on GOODLearningHub, which currently enrolls 73 students and
serves more than 400 youth daily across its full program mix.

### 4. Community Need and Alignment with FTE Priorities

Southwest DC saw a 25 percent increase in gun-related incidents in 2024, and
the families GOODProjects serves face compounding barriers of generational
poverty, housing insecurity, and chronic trauma exposure. Arts and cultural
access is not something these families' schools or neighborhoods otherwise
provide at the scale a professional DC theater or the Smithsonian can. The FTE
grant requires all funded activity to take place in DC and requires the
applicant to function as the producer of the experience rather than as an arts
organization itself; this proposal is built entirely around venues located
inside DC for exactly that reason. GOODProjects already runs a large, real
field trip program (see Part Three, Past Performance), but that existing
calendar is almost entirely outside DC, which is precisely the gap this grant
closes: a DC-based, arts-and-humanities-specific complement to programming
GOODProjects already knows how to plan, staff, and deliver safely at scale.

### 5. Target Population

30 GOODLearningHub youth, grades 6 through 8 (ages approximately 11 to 14),
drawn from James Creek, Syphax Gardens, and Greenleaf public housing in
Southwest DC's Ward 6. Approximately 95 percent Black or African American,
below 200 percent of the federal poverty line. The same 30-youth cohort
attends every trip in the series, so the unduplicated participant count is 30,
not a multiple of the trip count.

### 6. Program Design: The Field Trip Series

Four trips are confirmed as real, bookable, DC-based venues with content and
availability that fit inside the Oct 1, 2026 to Sep 30, 2027 grant window. A
fifth is flagged as a stretch addition pending date confirmation.

**Trip 1, Fall term (book between Oct 1 and Nov 1, 2026):** Step Afrika's *The
Fruits of Our Labor*, Arena Stage's Fichandler Stage, 1101 6th St SW (Ward 6).
Runs Sept 10 to Nov 1, 2026; the matinee must be booked on or after Oct 1,
2026, so the expense falls inside the grant period. Thematically tied to
America's 250th anniversary and, in the production's own language, "rhythm,
resilience, and the unfinished story of the American experience."

**Trip 2, Winter term (book within Dec 4 to 23, 2026, avoiding Dec 17):** Step
Afrika's *Magical Musical Holiday Step Show*, Arena Stage, same address. This
is the already-finalized anchor trip from the original single-trip
application. December 17, 2026 must be avoided: it is the last day of the
GOODLearningHub Fall II session and the date "Winter City Lights" is already
booked on GOODProjects' own recreational calendar for part of this cohort.

**Trip 3, Winter/Spring term (book between Oct 1, 2026 and May 14, 2027):**
Smithsonian National Portrait Gallery, 8th and F St NW (Ward 2). Free
admission. In-person student programs run Tuesday through Friday for exactly
this window, 75-minute standards-based gallery tours led by trained gallery
educators, maximum 60 students per visit. 2026-27 registration opens August 1,
2026.

**Trip 4, any term (exhibit runs through January 2028, so any date in the
grant period works):** Anacostia Community Museum, 1901 Fort Place SE (Ward
8). Free admission. Its "We Make History" exhibition, which opened May 30,
2026, is built around the same 250th-anniversary framing, drawing on the
museum's own collection including a diary written by Adam Francis Plummer, a
man who was enslaved in Prince George's County. Guided tours and artmaking
activities are offered for grades 6 through 8.

**Trip 5, stretch addition, Summer term (pending):** either a group visit to
the Smithsonian National Museum of African American History and Culture,
1400 Constitution Ave NW (Ward 2), free admission, the single most
directly on-theme venue in DC, requiring reservations well in advance; or
Dance Place's DanceAfrica DC festival, 3225 8th St NE (Ward 5), if 2027 dates
are announced in time (the 2026 festival ran June 1 to 7, 2026; 2027 dates
were not yet announced as of this research). This trip is not included in the
firm budget total below and should only be added once one of the two is
actually confirmed.

Each confirmed trip is paired with a pre-trip classroom workshop connecting the
venue's content to GOODLearningHub's existing social-emotional learning
curriculum, and a post-trip workshop. The two Arena Stage trips additionally
include a staff professional development session; Trip 2 includes the
on-site Arena Stage "Interactive" add-on already built into the finalized
anchor budget.
