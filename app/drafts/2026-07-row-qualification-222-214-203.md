## Row 222 — OESE Comprehensive Centers Program (Assistance Listing 84.283B): NO-GO

**Why:** Entity eligibility failure. The FY2026 Notice Inviting Applications limits
eligible applicants to "research organizations, institutions, agencies, institutions
of higher education (IHEs), or partnerships among such entities... with the
demonstrated ability or capacity to carry out" technical-assistance activities for
state and local educational agencies. This program funds three national/regional
Content Centers that provide technical assistance *to* school systems; it is not a
direct-service grant a community nonprofit like GOODProjects can apply for on its
own.

**Also:** the due date logged in the tracker (June 30, 2026) has already passed as
of today (July 14, 2026), so this specific competition is closed regardless of the
eligibility question.

**Recommendation:** skip. If Neo believes GOODProjects has a real partnership with
an eligible IHE or research organization that would let us participate as a
subcontractor, that changes the calculus, but there's no evidence of that here.

## Row 214 — Bell Textron Corporate Grant (CSI): HOLD, verify before drafting

**Why:** Not a clean disqualification, but the application window status is
unclear. Bell's own corporate responsibility page states the 2026 grant
application window is closed, with the portal reopening in spring for the next
cycle, which does not match the July 30, 2026 due date logged in the tracker.
Eligibility itself looks workable: 501(c)(3) status held 3+ years (GOODProjects
has held it since 2016), and Bell's giving priorities include STEM education and
health & human services, both of which have a real GOODProjects program to point
to (GOODLearningHub's STREAM component; FSP/GOODZone crisis response). No
geographic restriction was found in what's published, but Bell's major sites are
concentrated in Texas and other manufacturing hubs, so a DC-based applicant with
no facility connection may face the same kind of scrutiny that sank Corning.

**Recommendation:** before drafting, confirm directly with Bell's community
relations contact whether there is in fact an open window right now and whether
DC-based nonprofits with no local Bell facility are eligible. Sinking narrative
time into a possibly-closed portal is the wrong sequence; the Corning file
(`../drafts/2026-batch-triage-notes.md`, row 200) recommends the same
verify-first approach for a similar-shaped opportunity.

## Row 203 — DC Department of Behavioral Health, Peer Operating Centers: NO-GO

**Why:** Program mismatch. The identified RFA (RM0 POC082225, Peer Operating
Centers) funds a comprehensive care management program specifically for DC
residents with opioid use disorder (OUD) and/or stimulant use disorder (STUD),
requiring applicants to retain a Peer Outreach Coordinator to run the program.
GOODProjects does not operate a substance-use peer-recovery service; none of the
four core programs (FSP, GOODLearningHub, GOODZone, JUMPSTART) are peer-operated
OUD/STUD care management. This would be a forced fit of the kind Part 5 of the
playbook flags as a systemic defect, applying to a funder with no matching
program.

**Also:** the specific RFA cycle located (release August 22, 2025; LOI due
August 29, 2025; application deadline September 19, 2025) closed almost a year
ago. If DBH has a newer Peer Operating Centers cycle open now, it would need a
different program design than anything currently in the portfolio to qualify.

**Recommendation:** skip, unless Neo knows of a specific DBH relationship or
program capacity (e.g., an actual peer-recovery service GOODProjects runs or
plans to run) that isn't reflected in the current program reference docs.
