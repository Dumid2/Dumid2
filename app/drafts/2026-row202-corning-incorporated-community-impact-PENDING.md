# GOODProjects Grant Qualification Note
Row 202, 2026 dev bible tab: Corning Incorporated Community Impact and Investment

## Decision: GEOGRAPHY UNCERTAIN — pursue via introductory email, not a proposal draft (revised after deeper check)

## Why

Corning's Community Impact and Investment grants are restricted to
community-based programs within a **20-mile radius of a Corning business
location with at least 50 employees**. An initial pass on this
opportunity concluded DC had no qualifying Corning presence, but a
deeper check of Corning's own published worldwide-locations list found
that is not accurate. Corning has two real locations in the DC area:

- **Corning Incorporated Government Affairs Office**, 325 7th Street NW,
  Suite 600, Washington, DC 20004 — **about 1.1 miles** from
  GOODProjects' 996 Maine Avenue SW address, calculated directly from the
  two street addresses. Comfortably inside the 20-mile radius.
- **Corning Optical Communications Wireless, Inc.**, 13221 Woodland Park
  Rd, Suite 400, Herndon, VA 20171 — **about 20.5 miles** straight-line
  from GOODProjects, right at the edge of the radius (driving distance or
  Corning's own measurement method could put it just inside).

So the geographic question is not "no Corning presence near DC," it is
narrower: **does either location have at least 50 employees.** Public
sources don't settle this. The Government Affairs office is likely too
small — LinkedIn/The Org list Corning's entire federal government
affairs team at roughly 10 people company-wide, which suggests this
single office is well under 50. The Herndon Optical Communications
office's headcount could not be confirmed from public data; estimates
found online were inconsistent and not reliable enough to cite either
way.

This is exactly the determination Corning's own intake process exists
to make. The program is **invitation-only**: eligible organizations
introduce themselves by emailing philanthropy@corning.com, and Corning
decides internally whether the applicant clears both the radius and the
employee-count bar before inviting a full application. There is no
record of GOODProjects having done this yet.

Note: an earlier triage pass on this same opportunity is recorded in
`2026-batch-triage-notes.md` under row 200 in the dev bible tab at that
time; the row number appears to have shifted to 202 in a later revision
of the tracker. The underlying funder is unchanged.

## What was found

- **Focus areas:** arts and culture, STEM education, small business
  development, workforce support, and local DEI efforts.
- **Geographic requirement:** within 20 miles of a Corning site with
  50+ employees; the Corning/Elmira, NY window runs February 15 to
  September 15, and other business-location applications (which would
  include a DC-area site) run on the same February 15 to September 15
  window, with some organizations required to submit by May 30.
- **Eligibility:** 501(c)(3) or 509(a) public charity, no written
  discrimination policy.
- **Not funded:** event sponsorships, conferences, individuals,
  religious or political causes.
- **Entry point:** an introductory email to philanthropy@corning.com,
  not a direct application. Corning determines eligibility and issues
  an invitation before any proposal is accepted.

## Recommendation

Do not write a full proposal yet, since Corning won't accept one
without an invitation regardless of quality. Instead, send the
introductory email to philanthropy@corning.com now: note GOODProjects'
address (996 Maine Avenue SW), and ask directly whether the DC
Government Affairs office or the Herndon, VA Optical Communications
office qualifies as a 50+-employee business location for Community
Impact and Investment purposes. That single email resolves the open
question far faster than continued public research can, and if Corning
confirms eligibility, this becomes a real go with a much stronger
starting position than the original triage note assumed.
