## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The Carlson Family Foundation's Vibrant Communities priority funds inclusive access to arts, culture, and green spaces that affirm youth identity. GOODProjects requests $15,400 to expand SW Peace Gardens' therapeutic gardening and healing-centered community events alongside GOODSports afterschool programming for the 2026-2027 program year. This is a strong programmatic match on paper. It is not a strong match geographically: GOODProjects serves Washington, DC, not the Twin Cities, and this application cannot be submitted honestly without that gap being resolved first.

## ORGANIZATIONAL OVERVIEW

GOODProjects is a Washington, DC nonprofit (founded 2016) working to end the cycle of intergenerational poverty through a place-based, two-generation model, pairing children's education and youth development with the economic mobility, food security, health, and stability of the adults around them, so that progress in one generation reinforces the next. We serve residents of James Creek, Syphax Gardens, and Greenleaf, three public housing communities in Southwest DC's Ward 6, where the median household income is approximately $14,000.

GOODProjects operates with a staff of 30 (11 full-time, 19 part-time) and 50 volunteers. The organization has invested more than $8 million in Southwest DC since 2016. Our CEO Darius Baxter (Forbes 30 Under 30, 2023 Washingtonian of the Year) brings deep personal connection to the mission alongside executive leadership experience. Our 2024 audit by Desai Company returned an unmodified opinion (signed September 7, 2025).

## COMMUNITY NEED

Southwest DC's Ward 6 public housing communities, James Creek, Syphax Gardens, and Greenleaf, are among the city's most resource-constrained neighborhoods. The families GOODProjects serves face overlapping, mutually reinforcing challenges: limited employment pathways, fragile housing, food insecurity, underfunded schools, health disparities, and the compounding trauma of community violence. Addressing any one of these in isolation does not produce lasting change. GOODProjects addresses them together.

Youth in these communities have limited access to green, restorative spaces and structured recreational programming, both of which affirm identity and build resilience in ways that classroom instruction alone cannot. SW Peace Gardens and GOODSports were built to meet that specific gap.

## PROJECT DESCRIPTION

### Program Anchor

SW Peace Gardens and GOODSports together match the Foundation's Vibrant Communities priority: inclusive access to arts, culture, and green spaces that affirm youth identity.

### Target Population

GOODProjects serves Black families living in Ward 6 public housing, James Creek, Syphax Gardens, and Greenleaf. Youth ages 5 to 14 participate in GOODSports through GOODLearningHub, while SW Peace Gardens programming reaches youth and families across the broader community through healing-centered events.

### Activities and Approach

SW Peace Gardens delivers trauma-informed therapeutic gardening and healing-centered community events at gardens in Southwest DC, established in direct response to community violence. GOODSports uses football (soccer), basketball, and dance as life-skills vehicles within GOODLearningHub's afterschool programming, reaching 50 to 100 youth. This request would fund expanded garden programming hours, event supplies, and coaching stipends for GOODSports across the 2026-2027 program year.

## EQUITY STATEMENT

We operate on the principle that economic mobility requires addressing both skills gaps and systemic barriers simultaneously. We practice equity by eliminating barriers rather than asking people to overcome them. Our hiring, programming, and partnerships prioritize those with lived experience of the challenges we address. We actively recruit staff and board members from the communities we serve, recognizing that proximity to the problem creates proximity to the solution. We do not design programs for communities; we design them with communities.
