## Pre-Trip Workshop: "Stepping Into Story"

**Grade band:** 6th-8th grade
**Duration:** 45 minutes
**Facilitator:** GOODLearningHub staff (Stephanie Page-Baxter and/or Phileke Holland)
**Setting:** GOODLearningHub afterschool session, King Greenleaf Recreation Center

**Objectives.** By the end of this session, students will be able to:
1. Describe the historical origins of stepping as an African American cultural tradition rooted
   in HBCU life and Black Greek-letter organizations.
2. Connect the themes of identity, community, and collective voice in stepping to their own lived
   experience.
3. Generate at least one question or observation to carry into the field trip.

**Materials:** projector/screen, vocabulary handout, discussion journal, sample audio/video clip
of stepping if available.

**Sequence:**
1. *Warm-up / Do Now (5 min).* Journal prompt: "What is one tradition in your family or community
   that gets passed down? How do you learn it, and who taught you?"
2. *Direct instruction (15 min).* Brief history of stepping: its origins in African diasporic
   rhythm and movement traditions, its formalization within Black Greek-letter organizations at
   HBCUs, and its evolution into professional companies such as Step Afrika!. Key vocabulary:
   tradition, ensemble, rhythm, call-and-response, legacy.
3. *Guided discussion (15 min).* Peace-circle format (GOODLearningHub's existing SEL structure),
   connecting stepping's themes of discipline, unity, and collective voice to students' own
   experience of community and belonging.
4. *Preview and prompt-setting (10 min).* Preview what students will see at Arena Stage. Each
   student writes one question or thing they want to notice during the performance, to revisit in
   the post-trip session.

**Curriculum alignment:** connects directly to GOODLearningHub's existing Social-Emotional
Learning curriculum (identity, community, collective voice) and to CAH's Field Trip Experiences
goal of linking classroom learning to the field trip content itself.

## Post-Trip Reflection: "What Stepping Taught Us"

**Grade band:** 6th-8th grade
**Duration:** 45 minutes
**Facilitator:** GOODLearningHub staff (Stephanie Page-Baxter and/or Phileke Holland)
**Setting:** GOODLearningHub afterschool session, King Greenleaf Recreation Center

**Objectives.** By the end of this session, students will be able to:
1. Recall and describe specific moments from the performance.
2. Connect a theme from the performance (identity, community, tradition) to their own life or
   community.
3. Produce a piece of written or creative reflection that can be used as both a learning artifact
   and program evaluation evidence.

**Sequence:**
1. *Warm-up / Recall (5 min).* Quick-write: describe one moment from the performance that stood
   out and why.
2. *Small-group discussion (15 min).* Students revisit the questions/observations they wrote
   before the trip, and share and discuss them in small groups using the same peace-circle format.
3. *Reflection activity (15 min).* Student choice: (a) a short written reflection connecting a
   theme from the performance to their own life, or (b) a short piece of creative writing or
   artwork inspired by stepping's rhythms and themes.
4. *Closing share-out (10 min).* Volunteers share with the full group. The facilitator collects
   reflections both as a learning artifact and as evaluation evidence.

**Evaluation tie-in:** this session directly produces both halves of the evaluation plan already
in the narrative. The facilitator records whether each student's reflection connects the
production's themes to a personal or SEL discussion topic (the existing 75%-target quantitative
metric), and the collected written/verbal reflections themselves serve as the qualitative
evidence CAH's Addendum A expects alongside quantitative measures.
